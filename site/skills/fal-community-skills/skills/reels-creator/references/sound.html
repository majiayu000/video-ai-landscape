Sound: voice first, then the bed, then a few effects

The voice carries the video. Everything else sits under it and never competes.

Targets for the finished mix (host_reel.py mixes to them; mix_check.py measures them):

Target
Integrated loudness −14 LUFS
True peak ≤ −1 dBFS (loudnorm at −1.5)
Voice over the bed 15–25 dB (RMS while speaking vs in the pauses)

1. Music bed

Score it to the cut, not the other way round. 1. Run host_reel.py --plan first, with the end card already in the edit. The segment starts become the sections: intro, each scene, each host appearance, the close, the end card. 2. Write music.json with one section per part, each with a mood that fits its picture. 3. Call elevenlabs/music/v2.5 with a composition plan of one chunk per section: - {text: "[name]", duration_ms, positive_styles, negative_styles, context_adherence: "high"}; - the first chunk carries the global styles plus its mood; - later chunks carry their mood, "flows continuously from the previous section with no pause and no hard stop", and the same instruments, key and tempo; - the last chunk is the end card's: its positive styles add "resolves to the home chord" and "ends on a final held chord that rings out", and it bans an abrupt ending and a fade-out; the others ban endings, final chords and fades; - output_format is mp3_48000_192. It costs $0.60 a minute: say the cost first (Gate 5).

Instruments: - Use warm acoustic or soft electric: felt piano, upright or soft electric bass, brushed kit, strings, pizzicato, soft pads that breathe. - Never use synth leads, hi-hat rolls, risers, whooshes, cymbal swells, or bright ringing tones. They read as shrill and digital, above all on phone speakers. - Put the bans in global_neg.

Behaviour: - The music never cuts to silence mid-scene. It flows across sections: every later chunk says "flows continuously" and repeats the anchor. - It ends on a resolution, with the video. The last section is the end card's: a short cadence to the home chord, then a final held chord that rings out to the end of the video. Only that chunk may end; the ones before it ban endings and fades (step 3 above). - The bed comes back up by itself once the voice stops, and the card's "music_db" lifts it further (−14 over a bed at −20), so the ending is heard. Never fade the music out under the last word: the video then has no close.

In the edit: "music": {"file": "audio/bed.mp3", "from": 0, "db": -20, "duck_db": -25, "fade_in": 0.4, "fade_out": 1.0}. The bed ducks under the voice by itself. fade_out only softens the last second of the ring-out.

2. Clip sound

  • FLUX 3 Max clips come with their own sound (generate_audio defaults to true). host_reel keeps it as ambience: ambience_db −14 and duck_db −24 for 16:9, −12 and −22 for 9:16.
  • Host clips' audio is discarded. An H3 Max Lip Sync clip carries the take resampled to 32 kHz; the mix uses the take itself (voice-and-host.md §4).
  • H3 Max clips: their own audio track is seldom what a scene needs. Prefer Mirelo for their sound.
  • A b-roll clip with no or wrong sound: use Mirelo video-to-audio.
  • Set duration to the clip's length (the default is 10 s), num_samples: 1, a text_prompt naming off-screen sounds, and a negative_prompt such as "music, whoosh, riser, swell, bright ring".
  • Or use video-to-video to get the clip back with its sound mixed in.

3. SFX for animation scenes

Few and soft. A scene can carry one or two sounds that belong to its objects: - a soft tick as a chip lands; - a paper slide as a card docks; - a low thud as a big number settles.

No sound on every label, and none on the circle reveals.

Make them: - Mirelo text-to-audio with prompt (not text_prompt), a short duration, num_samples: 1 (or 2 when the user wants a choice), and the same negative prompt. - Or Mirelo video-to-audio on the rendered scene MP4, with a text_prompt naming the actions. It finds the motion by itself.

Screen them by ear and by spectrum (ffmpeg showspectrumpic in the local workspace). Reject sweeps (a rising or falling band) and bright, ringing highs.

Place them in the edit's sfx list, at segment-relative times taken from the scene's own timeline (wordtimes.py and the scene's seek times):

"sfx": [{"file": "sfx/chip_tick.wav", "seg": 3, "at": 4.62, "db": -20}, {"file": "sfx/dock.wav", "seg": 5, "at": 13.40, "db": -18, "duck": true}]

4. Checks

python3 ./project-work/tools/mix_check.py ./project-work/out/Video.mp4 ./project-work/out/Video_plan.json          # voice vs pauses, LUFS, true peak
python3 ./project-work/tools/review_sheet.py ./project-work/out/Video.mp4 ./project-work/out/Video_plan.json ./project-work/out/Video_sheet.jpg

Then listen to the whole video once, from start to end: - the first word of each host appearance; - the music under the first scene after a cut; - every SFX in context; - the ending: the card plays out and the music resolves on it, with no fade under the last word and no hard stop.

Numbers don't catch a shrill sound; ears do.
