Script Writer

Revision 2026-10-28a. Name this revision in the delivery; an upload that cannot quote it is an older copy of this skill.

A script for generated video is not a screenplay. Every shot is synthesised independently by a model that has never seen the shot before it, so an unbroken take, an "as previously", or a page of two-hander dialogue are all instructions nothing downstream can execute. What ships is a shot table: discrete generatable rows, a duration on each, a word-counted voiceover line, and an explicit statement of what carries from the row above. Two modes, chosen once and never mixed — Cinematic (acts, a turn, a resolution) and Ad (beats, a clock, an offer) — and both feed fal-video-production when the ask is a whole assembled ad, or fal-video-generation (one row, one clip), fal-audio-generation (one clip per row, length-constrained) when it is not; the continuity column stays in the table (a dedicated blocking skill is not published).

What makes this different from writing for camera

  1. Discrete shots, or nothing usable. Write each row so it could be generated by someone who has read only that row; anything it needs from earlier — a face, a jacket, a kitchen, the time of day — is named again inside it. "Continues from before" generates a different scene.
  2. Timing is arithmetic, not feel. Words per second is a number. Budget the script before writing a line of it (Step 1); hold every row to its cap.
  3. On-camera dialogue is the expensive way to say anything. Lip-sync is the least reliable thing a video model does, and it spends a whole clip on a talking head. Default to VO over visuals; reserve on-camera speech for a line whose value is the performance, and give it its own row.

Step 0 — Intake, once

One bundled ask_user_questions call for intake (if your host has no question tool, ask the same questions in one plain message, then in the same turn take the card's stated no-answer action and say so), covering all five items below. If the brief already answers everything, make zero calls and go to Step 1. Conditional cards that fire later have their own budget — see the card roster at the end of this step.

  1. Mode (header Mode) — Cinematic / narrative or Ad / commercial.
  2. Target duration (header Length) — 15s, 30s, 60s, 90s, or free text in seconds.
  3. Voice (header Voice) — VO over visuals (default), On-camera dialogue, or No words — visuals and music only.
  4. Delivery aspect (header Aspect) — 16:9, 9:16, 1:1.
  5. The brief (free text) — product or premise, audience, tone, must-say lines, and any claim the user wants stated.

Step 0b — Read the brief back before you write it

A brief can be perfectly clear and still not fit. Do this before writing a line, and report it in the reply whether or not anything is wrong — a triage that only speaks up on failure trains the reader to skip it.

Four questions, in this order:

  1. Does the beat list fit the shot budget? Count the distinct visual beats the brief asks for — every named location change, every new object introduced, every escalation step. Compare that to the shot budget from Step 1 — target ÷ 4s at default pacing, more when a fast-paced brief buys short cuts. Report the ratio. Over-subscription is the most common brief defect and it is invisible until assembly: a 30-second piece naming twelve beats has seven shots to hold them, so five beats will be dropped by someone, and it should be the author rather than whoever renders it. Name which beats you would merge or cut, in priority order, and why.
  2. Is every structurally required moment actually a shot? A piece that turns on a change — a transformation, a reveal, a before-and-after — needs the causing moment to be its own shot, and needs the shots either side to make the change legible. If the brief names the cause but the budget leaves no room to build to it, say so: the cause will read as arbitrary, and no amount of execution fixes a pivot that had nothing leading into it.
  3. Does anything the brief insists on have no room at all? A character who must appear, a product that must be seen, a line that must be said. Each needs a shot or a window. If the budget is already full, that requirement is in conflict with the beat list, and the author chooses — not you, silently.
  4. What is genuinely underspecified? Not "vague" — unexecutable. A brief can set a mood in one line and be entirely shootable. What matters is anything a row cannot be written without: what the thing looks like, where it is, what physically happens, and what carries from the row before.

Then, if any answer is a conflict, ask once. One card, header Scope, declared in the roster at the end of this step. Put your recommended cut list in the question text and offer Use your cuts / Extend the runtime / I'll revise the brief. With no answer, take your own cut list and proceed — the beats you dropped and the reason are then part of the delivery, not a silent loss. Never stall a script on this; an over-subscribed brief still produces a shootable piece, it just produces a shorter one than the author imagined.

When you have to invent. Count how many of these four the brief leaves open: the product's benefit, the character's want, the offer, a claim. That list is exhaustive — tone and audience are style choices the defaults table already covers, not inventions. Two or fewer: invent, write, list them at delivery as assumptions. Three or more, or an invented benefit or offer: raise the Assumptions card — its text your drafted list in full, options Use these / Change them. Unanswered, proceed with the drafted list and mark it unconfirmed at delivery.

Every card this skill can ever raise, declared here. Ask with a card (on a host with no question tool, one plain message, then in the same turn take the card's stated no-answer action and say so); never treat an unanswered message as an answer. The first row is the intake bundle above; the other four are conditional: each fires only when its condition holds, and none of them blocks, because its row states the action taken when no answer comes, so the pipeline never stalls on a card.

Step 1 — The word budget, before a word is written

Speaking rate, words per second: 2.3 measured/premium/sombre · 2.8 conversational — the default, and roughly what TTS returns at default speed · 3.3 urgent retail or fast comedy, above which you cut copy instead. Apply a 0.9 headroom factor so lines land inside their shot rather than butting the cut, making every budget here floor(seconds x rate x 0.9) — applied to the whole script and to each shot's Cut. At the 2.8 default: 30s carries 75 words, total. 15s carries 37. 60s carries 151. An 8-second shot carries 20. A 2-second hook carries five.

Compute it in one sandbox pass, never by eye — one mechanism, so the numbers in the delivered table are the numbers you checked:

rate, cuts = 2.8, [2.0, 4.0, 10.0]          # cut seconds, one per shot
print([int(s * rate * 0.9) for s in cuts], sum(cuts), int(sum(cuts) * rate * 0.9))

Cut length is not generated length

A model generates in the lengths its endpoint allows, not the length your beat wants. Verified against the live schemas: H3 Max, the default route, takes a number of seconds from 0.92 to 15 (decimals allowed; the output can run about 0.7s longer than asked), so a 2-second hook is generated at 2 seconds. Most fallbacks floor higher (Wan 3.0 Prime takes integers from 2): Seedance 2.5, the cross-family fallback, takes whole seconds 4 to 30 (bare integers as strings — "4", not "4s"; Seedance 2.0 stops at 15); Veo 3.1 Fast takes only 4s, 6s or 8s and defaults to 8s; Kling v3 Pro takes 3 to 15, default 5; base MiniMax H3 takes integers 5 to 15. On those routes a 2-second hook is generated at the route's shortest allowed length and trimmed in the edit.

Pacing sets your shot budget; the route floor prices it. At default pacing Cut ≈ Gen at about 4 seconds a shot, so a 30-second piece holds about 7 shots. A fast-paced brief — TikTok, frenetic, montage — is allowed to break that ceiling the way the hook already does: every row cut at 1–2.5 seconds. On H3 Max Gen can then match Cut; on a fallback route each row is generated at the route's minimum, so eight quick shots in a 15-second ad bill about 32 generated seconds on Seedance or Veo. Either way the budget is set by cost rather than arithmetic, so report the generated total in the delivery and let the shot count follow the brief's energy. A beat list that exceeds even that is cut by the author in Step 0b, never silently at assembly.

Default to the route fal-video-generation actually uses, which is H3 Max — do not re-derive a route from this paragraph. Rows with photoreal people still plan on H3 Max; a shot H3 Max refuses moves to Veo 3.1 Fast (4s/6s/8s) per fal-video-generation's moderation rule, so say in the delivery that such a row's Gen may rise to the next Veo length (4s, 6s or 8s; 8s for a reference row) at Veo 3.1 Fast's rate, so quote that cost too; a row that fails fal-video-generation's Veo fit test goes back to the user if refused. This skill picking a different one silently overrides the video skill's whole routing table: a run whose rows were planned against Veo's three-value enum came back 4/4/8/8/6/4 for a 2/4/8/8/5/3 contract and had to be rescued by trimming, purely because this step named Veo first. Choose otherwise only for a stated reason — a row cut longer than 8s excludes Veo; a row that must render under 4s rules out Seedance and Veo, under 5s also rules out base H3, and under 6s rules out LTX 2.5 — and say which route you took and why, so the video skill can honour it rather than re-decide.

Every row therefore carries two numbers: Cut, what the finished video uses and what must sum to target, and Gen, what to ask the video skill for, with Gen >= Cut always and Gen a length the route actually offers.

One rule for choosing Gen, not three. Take the shortest allowed length that is at least Cut, on the route the video skill will use. Confirm the route's allowed lengths from its schema rather than from this paragraph — these change, and an earlier draft of this skill asserted Veo was fixed at 8 seconds, which the schema disproves. If the route is genuinely undecided, pick the candidate whose allowed lengths cover your longest Cut — Veo 3.1 Fast caps at 8s, so any row cut longer than that excludes it — then take every row's Gen from that one route's schema, and say which routes you checked.

Step 2A — Cinematic mode

Write four one-sentence lines first — the reason the shots are in that order, and part of the delivery.

  • Premise — who, where, and what is about to be disturbed.
  • Want — what the character is chasing, visible in action.
  • Need — what they actually lack. Want and need must not be the same thing.
  • Turn — the single moment the want stops working. Name the shot number.

Then shape the rows: setup (the world, plus the want made physical) → pressure (the want fails once) → the turn → escalation (a costlier attempt) → resolution (the need answered, or refused). At 60s that is roughly 8s / 16s / 8s / 16s / 12s; scale proportionally. Under 30 seconds drop escalation, not the turn — a piece with no turn is a mood reel. Cast two speaking characters at most, each with a one-word ALL-CAPS tag reused verbatim in every row, so staging can be read off the table as-is.

Symptom in the draft What is actually wrong Structural fix
Every row is the same temperature no turn; the want never fails show the want working in row 1, then break it at the midpoint
The last row explains itself in VO the need was never dramatised give the resolution a physical action that only reads if the need was met — then delete the line
The middle rows are interchangeable repetition posing as escalation each attempt costs more than the last; raise the price, not the volume
Two characters talk for three rows dialogue is carrying the plot convert to VO over visuals, keep at most one on-camera line
Nothing can be cut to reach target two premises in one piece delete the second premise, never the turn

Step 2B — Ad mode

Six beats, budgeted at 30s and rate 2.8. At any other length: drop the beats the Cut it when column names, scale each surviving Cut by target / 30 in whole seconds — trimming or padding Demo so the column still sums exactly — then recompute every Words cap with the Step 1 formula. When supplied verbatim copy fixes a row's word count instead, invert that arithmetic and derive the duration from the words: Cut = ceil(W / (rate x 0.9)).

Beat Job 30s cut Words Cut it when Fold into
Hook Stop the scroll. One image, one idea. 2s 5 never —
Problem The cost of not having it, shown not stated. 4s 10 length ≤ 15s the hook, as the "before" half
Demo The product doing the thing. The only mandatory beat. 10s 25 never —
Proof Why believe it — but see Step 3. 6s 15 no substantiated claim exists drop entirely, extend Demo
Offer What to do, where, by when. 5s 12 brand film with no CTA the tagline card
Tagline Logo, line, hold. 3s 7 never —

Durations sum to 30s; words sum to 74, inside the 75-word budget.

Beats are not shots. At fast pacing — TikTok, frenetic — a beat, usually Demo, splits into several rows of 1–2.5 second cuts (a montage), each generated at its Cut on H3 Max (at the route floor and trimmed on a fallback), each row still one action with its anchors restated. The beat's words divide across its rows and the caps apply per row.

When verbatim copy consumes the clock at full length — nothing in Cut it when fires, yet must-say words leave no room for six beats — verbatim copy outranks beat count. Fold beats using their own Fold into cells, in the order Problem, then Proof, then Offer, until the remaining rows fit, and name every fold in the delivery.

The hook is not an intro. Five words and one legible image; a logo sting in the first two seconds spends the hook on a beat the tagline already owns.

# Beat Cut Gen Visual (shot seed) VO W On-screen text Carries in
1 Hook 2s 2s Overhead macro, matte-black kettle on pale oak, a single drip hangs off the spout and falls into a white cup. Locked off, no camera move. Ninety seconds. That's it. 4 90 SECONDS lower third opens the film — nothing carries in. PRODUCT matte-black kettle and SURFACE pale oak established here and restated in every later row

Step 3 — Continuity, and claims

The Carries in column is the only continuity that exists. Write it as named, describable properties — WARDROBE, LOCATION, PROP, TIME, PALETTE, character tag — each restated inside that row's shot seed. Two tags may depend on something stronger, and both are planned, never assumed:

  • FRAME — starts on the previous row's last frame. Downstream extracts that frame and passes it as the start image to an image-to-video route. Cap at one FRAME link per script unless the user asks for more.
  • MAP — two or more characters whose positions must not swap. Record per row each character's screen side, facing and eyeline in Carries in, and keep them identical across rows that cut together.

Anything else reading as "same as the last shot" is a defect; Step 5 greps for it.

Claims. An ad script puts words in a brand's mouth. Never invent a statistic, a percentage, a superlative (best, #1, fastest), a comparative (twice as long as), an award, a rating, a review or an endorsement — not as a placeholder, not as an example, not in on-screen text where it reads as fine print. If Proof has nothing substantiated to say, cut it per the beat table.

A claim the user typed is not a claim the user can make. Where supplied copy touches health, safety, medical, financial or environmental territory, or any superlative or comparative, it needs substantiation — and you make that call by reading the copy itself, never by reading an intake answer. Two sources satisfy it:

  • The brief asserts a sign-off — "legal signed off on this exact wording", "we hold the study". That counts as answered; record the assertion as the source in check 8.
  • The Claim substantiation card (header declared in Step 0), listing each such line verbatim, options We hold evidence for all of these / Remove them / I'll revise them.

An unsubstantiated claim does not ship — even verbatim, even must-say; substantiation outranks the must-say guardrail. With neither source, deliver the script with that line omitted, mark its row, list the omission in the delivery note, and raise the card so an answer can reinstate the line in a revision. Never stall the run waiting on the card.

Step 4 — Write the rows

  1. Fill Cut for every row and make the column sum to target before writing any copy — copy expands to fill the space it is given. Then compute each row's W cap with the Step 1 script.
  2. Write the VO line and count it. Over cap, cut words — never raise the rate, never lengthen the shot to fit the line.
  3. Write the shot seed as a self-contained visual: subject, wardrobe, location, light, framing, camera move. Present tense, one action, and everything the Carries in cell names restated in full. Find fal-shot-craft with search_guidance and load it with read_guidance (kind: "skill", with the returned id and version_id) for how to word it — slot-composed framing, a motivated or explicitly locked camera, the specific gesture rather than the named emotion — and print its validator lines per row. If it will not load, this item's minimums stand and the delivery says the craft reference was unavailable.
  4. Fill On-screen text (ad) with the literal string to burn, or —; under six words, echoing the VO rather than duplicating it. Then fill Gen.

Step 5 — Verification: arithmetic, not taste

Run all of these and answer each explicitly. Checks 6 to 9 are conditional; an inapplicable check is n/a, which is a pass.

  1. Every row has a numeric Cut. No blanks, no ranges, no "approx".
  2. Cut sums to the target, exactly, and report the sum. A 31s cut of a 30s spot is a fail, not a rounding.
  3. Every row has Gen >= Cut, and Gen is a length the route can produce.
  4. Per-row words <= floor(Cut x rate x 0.9). List every row and its cap; one overrun fails the check.
  5. Total VO words <= floor(target x rate x 0.9). Report both numbers.
  6. (any row depending on an earlier one) No Carries in cell says "continues", "same as above", "as before", or names the previous shot's final frame — unless tagged FRAME and planned in Step 3. Grep the Carries in column only; a whole-document grep flags the sentence reporting this check.
  7. (ad only) Hook Cut <= 2.0s and hook words <= 5, and no logo or brand name occupies row 1's visual. The restriction is the image only — the VO may name the brand, since a must-say line is allowed to open the film.
  8. (any claim present) Every number, superlative and comparative traces to a user-supplied line Step 3 records as substantiated — a sign-off asserted in the brief, or an answered Claim substantiation card. List them with their sources; a size, volume or count printed on the product counts as substantiated, with the label as its source; a printed performance, health or safety figure ("SPF 50", "24 h cold") still needs substantiation. A line Step 3 withheld appears nowhere, including on-screen text.
  9. (on-camera dialogue only) No row has two speaking characters, and every on-camera line sits in a row of at least 3s. Count the rows with dialogue: if it is most of them, the piece should have been VO.

Fix and re-run. If a script fails check 2 or 4 twice, the target length is wrong for the piece. Do not compress a third time: write the script at the next length up (15s → 30s → 60s → 90s), deliver it with the override flagged first in the delivery, and list what staying at the brief's length would have required cutting. This escalation needs no answer from anyone; when the cause is must-say overflow, the Must-say budget card is optional and additive on top of it.

Step 6 — Deliver

Hand back, in order: mode and target; the four cinematic lines or the beat budget; the filled table; the Step 5 numbers; then the hand-off. Name every field filled from the defaults table, and everything invented.

If the brief is a whole finished ad, hand off once, not three times. A request that wants clips and narration and music and on-screen text assembled into a delivered file goes to fal-video-production, with this table as its stage-1 output. Hand it the filled table and the Step 1 word budgets intact — it derives each shot's audio placement from the dur column, so the windows computed here are its contract, not a suggestion. Splitting the same brief across the three hand-offs below instead is how an ad ends up with clips nobody reviewed, narration bunched into the opening seconds and on-screen text that never got burned: each sibling does its own part correctly and no one owns the joins.

If fal-video-production will not load, take the hand-offs below and say plainly that nothing is then orchestrating them — the caller has to sequence the stages and burn any on-screen text last, after every re-render.

For a single shot, a single clip, or a script the user will assemble themselves, the individual hand-offs are correct:

  • → fal-video-generation — one row = one clip: shot seed, Gen, aspect.
  • → fal-audio-generation — the VO column one clip per row, each with that row's target window, so narration is generated to length rather than trimmed. Never hand it the column as a single continuous script: a one-piece read has to be cut apart afterwards by detecting silence, and it fails by crowding every word into the first seconds and leaving the tail silent.
  • MAP and FRAME rows travel with the clip hand-off: MAP rows keep their screen side, facing and eyeline in Carries in; for FRAME, extract the previous clip's last frame and pass it as the start image to an image-to-video route.

House rules that do not apply here: 2, 3, 5 and 11 govern endpoint schemas, optimise flags and sandbox uploads; 4 governs edit models redrawing assets; 15 and 19 measure change in a generated artifact. This skill submits nothing, names no parameters and generates nothing, so all of those land on the downstream skills — including the named-fallback rule: with no model calls there is no primary route to fall back from, so no fallback is named. 1, 6–10, 12, 13, 14 (the hand-offs above are pipeline hand-offs, not routing), 16, 17 and 18 are applied above.

Guardrails

  • A row a model cannot generate alone is not a row. If you cannot delete every other row and still generate this one, it is under-specified.
  • Two speaking characters, maximum, and never two in one row — crosstalk and overlapping dialogue do not survive generation.
  • Never put text in a shot seed. On-screen text lives in its own column and is burned in the edit; models render lettering unreliably, and a misspelled brand name in frame is a reshoot.
  • Must-say lines are load-bearing. They go in verbatim and count against the budget like everything else — never paraphrase one, never raise the rate to fit one, whatever the brief instructs. If they alone exceed the target's budget, the arithmetic decides on its own: report the numbers (their word count, the budget, the seconds they need at the stated rate), then take the Step 5 escalation — the next length up, every line intact, override flagged first in the delivery. Optionally raise the Must-say budget card (declared in Step 0; one option per line to cut, plus Go to the longer length), but never wait on it — unanswered, the longer script stands.
  • Not the clean case. A brief that is already a finished script → convert it to rows and run Step 5 on it, reporting failures rather than rewriting the voice. A mood with no product or character → invent nothing, go to the Assumptions card. Under 8 seconds → hook and tagline only, and say the other beats do not fit. More than about a dozen rows for 30 seconds at default pacing, or a generated total past roughly three times the finished length at fast pacing → the cutting is faster than generated clips hold up under; consolidate and say why.
